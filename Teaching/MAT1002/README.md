# MAT1002 · Calculus II

**Interactive learning resources for Calculus II**

Explore sequences, infinite series, and the geometry of curves through short visual experiments. Change one parameter, predict what will happen, and explain what you observe.

Start with sequences, continue to partial sums, and then explore Taylor polynomials. The curves and coordinates modules can be used independently alongside the corresponding course topics.

## Sequences and series {#sequences-and-series}

Build the connection between individual terms, partial sums, and polynomial approximation.

### Sequences and convergence

Plot a sequence from its formula, change the number of displayed terms, and adjust the epsilon band around an estimated limit.

**Try:** Compare `1/n` and `(-1)^n/n`. What changes when you make the epsilon band narrower?

[Open sequences module](Sequence.html)

### Infinite series and partial sums

Compare individual terms with their running partial sums. Choose a harmonic, geometric, or alternating example, or enter your own formula.

**Think about:** Why can the terms approach zero while the series still diverges?

[Open series module](Series2.html)

### Power series and Taylor polynomials

Change the degree of a Taylor polynomial centered at zero and compare it with a preset function, including the exponential, sine, cosine, geometric, and logarithmic examples.

**Try:** Increase the degree for `1/(1-x)`. Compare the approximation inside and outside the interval `(-1, 1)`.

[Open power series module](Power_Series.html)

## Curves and coordinates {#curves-and-coordinates}

Connect equations and parameters to the shapes they describe.

### Parametric curves in three dimensions

Enter `x(t)`, `y(t)`, and `z(t)`, choose a parameter interval, and inspect the resulting three-dimensional curve. Start with the helix example and change one component.

**Think about:** How does changing the parameter interval alter the part of the curve you see?

[Open parametric curves module](Parametrization.html)

### Cycloids and rolling curves

Move the rotation slider to trace a point on a rolling circle. Compare rolling along a line with rolling inside or outside a fixed circle, and vary the radius and tracing distance.

**Try:** In the linear mode, compare a tracing distance smaller than, equal to, and larger than the rolling radius.

[Open rolling curves module](Cycloid.html)

### Polar curves and regions

Compare two polar curves with separate angle intervals, or shade a region using radial and angular bounds.

**Think about:** How do the angle bounds determine which portion of a curve or region is drawn?

[Open polar coordinates module](Polar.html)

## How to use these modules {#study-guide}

- **Predict:** Write down the behavior you expect before changing a formula or parameter.
- **Explore:** Change one input at a time and compare the result with your prediction.
- **Explain:** Connect the picture to a definition, theorem, or calculation from the course.

The plots show finitely many computed values. In particular, the sequence module estimates its limit from the displayed terms. Use these pictures to form conjectures, then justify convergence or divergence mathematically.

These modules supplement the course. Consult the materials provided in class for the syllabus, assignments, and assessment arrangements.
